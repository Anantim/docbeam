=== The 523 s pause, explained ===

It is the longest quiet stretch between two completed deliveries, measured at the tail of the run. It is not a stop of
the system. Delivery logs, per minute (UTC):

  task delivered:  16:20 = 720   16:21 = 1148   16:22 = 124   16:24 = 7   16:25 = 3   16:32 = 1
  so 1992 of 2000 were done by 16:22:xx, within 90 s of the failover, and 8 stragglers followed.

What happened:
  1. 16:20:53 to 16:21:13, the database was gone for about 15 s. 1,264 delivery attempts failed on the database
     (1,236 ECONNREFUSED, 22 "connection timeout", 6 "Query read timeout" -- the new 20 s query timeout at work) and the
     Platform answered 500 to 21 requests between 16:20:55 and 16:21:10. Before the pool fix the Platform stayed broken
     for more than 10 minutes (run 1).
  2. Each failed message was put back with the retry gap for its receive count: 30 s, then 2 min, then 5 min, then 10 min.
     The first retry, 30 s later, found the database back, so almost every task finished in the 16:21 and 16:22 minutes.
  3. Tasks that failed twice waited the next gap (2 min): 7 delivered at 16:24. Tasks that failed a third time waited
     5 min: 3 delivered at 16:25.
  4. The last task was delivered at 16:32:49, about 714 s after the failover. That is the queue's 12-minute visibility
     timeout (720 s), not a retry gap: an attempt that was cut off by the failover without setting a retry gap returned
     to the queue only when its visibility timeout ran out. This is the most likely cause: the timing matches to the
     second, but that single task's own log lines were not traced.
  So: failover 20 s + retry gaps (30 s, 2 min, 5 min) for the stragglers + one 12-minute visibility wait = a quiet
  tail of 523 s. Nothing was lost and nothing was stored twice. Limit found: after a database failover a message can
  wait up to 12 minutes; a shorter queue visibility timeout would shorten that, at the cost of the 6 x rule of the design.
